\begin{abstract}
Small online shops in Bangladesh receive customer messages in Bangla, romanised
Bangla (``Banglish'') and English, and must decide which messages a system may
answer alone and which require a person. Order Khata is a 48-hour build of such a
triage system under a strict zero-cost constraint: one free container, CPU only,
no paid API. This proposal argues that the build contains the skeleton of a
publishable applied study, but not yet the study itself. The material supports
three candidate contributions: (i)~a full selective-automation analysis of a
shipped policy --- 53.6\% coverage at 94.2\% auto-slice accuracy at
$\tau=0.85$, with a measured structural ceiling of 56.2\%; (ii)~an empirical
result on bounded LLM deferral --- when the local model and the LLM disagree at
confidence $\geq 0.90$, the local model is right 96\% of the time against the
LLM's 23\%, which justifies a tie-breaker-only-when-uncertain design; and
(iii)~a cost--quality comparison of a 12\,ms linear model, a 322M-parameter
checkpoint, and an LLM API as first-class systems. The proposal lists the six
experiments, the statistics, and the human verification that separate the
current artifact from a Q1 submission, with an eight-week plan and a venue
ladder.
\end{abstract}

\section{Context and motivation}
Order Khata reads customer messages, classifies them into six classes
(order, price question, delivery problem, complaint, scam, other), and routes
each message to
one of three lanes: auto-handled, human review, or scam. The routing decision
is a five-rule policy over calibrated signals; it is implemented twice, in
Python and TypeScript, and parity-tested. The engineering budget is zero:
everything runs in one free container with a 512\,MiB RAM ceiling on one
deployment target, which excludes large local models and paid APIs alike.

The evaluation was disciplined for a hackathon: decision rules were fixed
before results were seen, thresholds were chosen on validation only, a coverage
target was corrected once measurement proved it unreachable rather than the
system being bent toward it, and every displayed number is read from a single
artifact (\texttt{backend/artifacts/eval.json}). The headline measurements are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
      \item the shipped linear engine reaches 0.820 accuracy and 0.816 macro-F1
      on 806 held-out test messages;
      \item at the default threshold $\tau=0.85$ the policy auto-handles 53.6\%
      of messages at 94.2\% accuracy on that slice;
      \item temperature scaling cuts expected calibration error from 0.200 to
      0.049 (15 bins);
      \item the scam head attains 96.9\% recall at a 0.25\% false-alarm rate on
      ordinary shop messages, with the threshold fixed on validation beforehand;
      \item the full signal path runs at p50 3.6\,ms / p95 4.6\,ms on CPU.
\end{itemize}

Applied papers in this space usually report a single accuracy figure for a
fine-tuned transformer. We believe (and the literature review in
Section~\ref{sec:related} will verify) three angles are under-explored for this
setting: cost as a first-class evaluation axis, the conditions under which an
LLM ``second opinion'' helps rather than harms, and the decomposition of
coverage ceilings in deferral policies. The domain itself --- code-switched
Bangla commerce messaging --- is under-served relative to English support
traffic.

\section{Research questions}
\begin{enumerate}[label=RQ\arabic*,leftmargin=*]
      \item \textbf{Cost--quality frontier.} Under a free-tier deployment
      budget, where do a character n-gram linear model, multilingual encoders,
      and LLM APIs sit on the accuracy--latency--memory Pareto frontier for
      six-class triage of code-switched messages?
      \item \textbf{Bounded LLM deferral.} Does an independent LLM second
      signal improve selective accuracy at fixed coverage, and does that
      improvement survive once the local model is well calibrated? When the two
      disagree, which one should be trusted as a function of local confidence?
      \item \textbf{Coverage ceiling.} How much of the non-automatable share of
      an inbox is structural (messages that ask for a person, messages the
      model is genuinely unsure about) versus improvable by a better model?
      \item \textbf{Confidence under shift.} How does per-script performance
      (Bangla, Banglish, English, mixed) and out-of-distribution behaviour
      interact with calibration, i.e.~is the confidence score still trustworthy
      where the model is weakest?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions claimed}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
      \item \textbf{C1 --- Selective-automation analysis of a shipped policy.}
      A complete coverage--accuracy curve over $\tau \in [0.50, 0.99]$ computed
      with the real routing function, plus a ceiling decomposition showing that
      a \emph{perfect} escalate oracle still caps coverage at 56.2\% because
      26\% of messages are not classified confidently and 13\% explicitly ask
      for a person. The ceiling argument turns a product limitation into an
      analytical result.
      \item \textbf{C2 --- Bounded LLM deferral, measured.} On the test split,
      cases where the LLM and the local model disagree at top probability
      $\geq 0.90$ favour the local model 96\% to 23\%. We use this to justify
      and formalise a veto design in which the LLM may only send a message to
      review, never decide alone, and only when the local model is uncertain;
      an unconditional veto is measured as the contrast condition and costs
      both coverage and accuracy.
      \item \textbf{C3 --- Cost as an evaluation axis.} The same task measured
      across a 12\,ms linear model, a 322M-parameter checkpoint (45.6\,ms,
      $\geq$3\,GiB host requirement with an observed out-of-memory failure below
      that), and a rate-limited free-tier LLM API --- including the refusal
      threshold that keeps a 512\,MiB host alive.
      \item \textbf{C4 (conditional) --- Verified evaluation slice.} A
      human-re-annotated subsample with inter-annotator agreement, if the
      annotation plan in Section~\ref{sec:data} is executed. Without it, C4 is
      dropped and the paper does not claim label quality beyond the source
      datasets.
\end{itemize}

Two attribution notes that any paper must keep: the 0.989 macro-F1 of the

\subsection{What is missing for a journal-grade study}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=*]
      \item Baseline depth: no fine-tuned mBERT/XLM-R/Bangla-specific encoder
      rows, and only one hosted LLM for the zero-shot row.
      \item Statistics: one split, no seeds, no confidence intervals, no
      significance tests; abstaining systems are compared on different subsets.
      \item Label verification: no inter-annotator agreement anywhere; the
      escalate flag is a policy annotation, not ground truth.
      \item OOD scale: $n=105$ with a judgement-call mapping is a caveat, not
      a result.
      \item LLM reproducibility: rows measured against a live API whose models
      rotate; the cache and model ids must be frozen before submission.
      \item Related work: the project currently contains no citations at all.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work (to be written)}
\label{sec:related}
The final paper will organise prior work by approach, not chronology; the four
strands below are the planned structure, each with the gap it must establish.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
      \item \textbf{Calibration and selective prediction.} Platt scaling~\cite{platt1999}
      provides the sigmoid correction lineage used for the binary heads, and
      Chow's reject-option work~\cite{chow1970} is the origin of trading errors against
      abstention. Temperature scaling~\cite{guo2017} established that modern networks need
      post-hoc scalar calibration, and selective-classification work~\cite{geifman2017} formalises
      accuracy-under-abstention, which is exactly the quantity our coverage
      curve reports; out-of-distribution behaviour is judged against detection baselines~\cite{hendrycks2017}. Our use is applied rather than methodological: the
      contribution is the measured behaviour of these tools on this task, not
      a new estimator.
      \item \textbf{LLM routing and cascades.} Judge-style evaluation~\cite{zheng2023} assumes
      the judge is reliable; routing and frugality work~\cite{chen2023,ong2024} reduces cost by
      sending easy cases to small models and hard cases to large ones, usually
      with preference data or a learned router. Our setting has neither budget
      for preference collection nor labels for a router: the design under test
      is a \emph{rule-based bounded veto} --- the LLM may only disagree the
      system into review, only below a decisive-confidence gate --- and we
      measure the contrast conditions (no veto, unconditional veto).
      \item \textbf{Multilingual and code-switched encoders.} Bidirectional
      pretraining~\cite{devlin2019} and its large-scale multilingual extension~\cite{conneau2020} underpin every
      candidate encoder row; Bangla-specific variants will be surveyed at
      execution time. The gap we test is per-script behaviour under a single
      deployed policy --- one operating point, four script modes --- rather
      than another pooled F1 score.
      \item \textbf{Bangla resources and fraud detection.} D1~\cite{d1} and D2~\cite{d2} are the
      task sources, and BanglaTOD~\cite{banglatod} supplies the only out-of-source check
      available today; SMS-phishing literature will be surveyed for metric
      conventions. The established gap is domain mismatch (SMS versus shop
      chat) plus corpus provenance (machine-generated text), which Section 7
      addresses by verification rather than by claim.
\end{itemize}
The gap paragraph will argue, subject to the review's outcome, that existing
evaluations report pooled accuracy on pooled test sets, while the operational
question --- \emph{what can be automated, at what confidence, at what cost,
under a free-tier budget} --- requires selective metrics, cost axes, and
per-script slices reported together.

\section{Proposed methodology}
\subsection{Data and verification plan}
First, re-run the existing pipeline end to end from pinned code and regenerate
\texttt{eval.json} with seeds recorded. Second, the annotation pilot: two
annotators independently label a stratified subsample of at least 300 messages
across class $\times$ script cells (including all low-confidence errors and a
matched high-confidence sample), Cohen's $\kappa$ is reported, disagreements
are adjudicated, and the adjudicated slice becomes the \emph{verified} test
subset on which headline numbers are repeated. If $\kappa$ is poor, that
result is itself a finding about the source labels and is reported as such.
Third, OOD expansion: at least 500 customer messages from at least two
sources outside D1 with a mapping validated by the same two annotators; if the
mapping proves unreliable, OOD stays qualitative, consistent with the existing
evaluation plan. Datasets with non-commercial licences are used for
non-commercial research only, and the licensing of any released subset is
checked before publication.

\subsection{Metrics and statistics}
Classification: accuracy, macro-F1, per-class and per-script values, each with
bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals (10{,}000 resamples). Pairwise system
comparisons use McNemar's test on identical subsets; seed-trained systems are
reported as mean $\pm$ std. Selective prediction: coverage, auto-slice
accuracy, and area under the risk--coverage curve, with all abstaining systems
compared at matched coverage rather than at their own operating points.
Calibration: ECE (15 bins), adaptive ECE, Brier score, and classwise ECE
before and after temperature scaling, with intervals. Fraud detection:
precision, recall, F1 on D2 positives against D1 shop-chat negatives, plus
false-alarm rate with an interval and recall at a fixed 1\% false-alarm rate.
Latency and peak memory: p50/p95 on the deployment host, not the workstation.
Ablations: veto off / bounded / unconditional $\times$ a sweep of the
decisive-confidence gate; escalate rule on/off; oracle-ceiling decomposition
of coverage; the scam invariant (nothing at or above the scam threshold may
enter the auto lane) tested as an assertion, not reported as a number.

\subsection{Reproducibility package}
An executed notebook with stored outputs, pinned environment files, the frozen
LLM response cache with model ids and dates, seeds, figures regenerated from
the notebook, and the evaluation script that produces \texttt{eval.json} from
raw data in one command.

\section{Honest limitations and compliance}
The paper inherits the artifact's thirteen-item limitations list verbatim in
substance: machine-generated intent corpus; machine-assisted gold labels;
SMS-style fraud data; single-source in-distribution test with a small OOD
check; simulated inbox with no live platform integration; class-granularity
decisions; engine attribution for every reported number. Framing rules also
carry over: the corpus is a \emph{public Bangla e-commerce customer-message
corpus}, never ``real seller messages''; no accuracy figure is hardcoded
anywhere in the product; and the bibliography in this proposal is provisional
and will be verified field by field before submission. Authorship practice
follows the project's rule that an AI coding harness did substantial work: the
venue's AI-disclosure policy will be checked and complied with, and the
contribution claims here are proposals for measurement, not measurements, until
the re-run in W1 produces them.

\section{Deliverables}
(1)~Workshop paper, weeks 1--8, on C1--C3 with the re-run evidence;
(2)~reproducibility package (executed notebook, cache, seeds, one-command
artifact regeneration); (3)~journal extension with the verified slice and
expanded OOD, targeted at the Q2/Q3 or Q1 tier depending on what the data
supports; (4)~if and only if C4 survives annotation, release of the verified
subset under cleared licences.

\section{Conclusion}
The proposal does not claim that the existing artifact is a paper; it claims
that the artifact already contains three measurable contributions and an
unusually honest evaluation protocol, and that six specific experiments plus
statistical and human verification convert it into one. The gate for
continuing is cheap: W1's re-run either reproduces the numbers or ends the
project early.
